Sorting

Sorting is the process of arranging data in a specific order, such as ascending (smallest to largest) or descending (largest to smallest). It helps organize data, making it easier to search, analyze, and process.

Types of Sorting
Ascending Order: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5
Descending Order: 5, 4, 3, 2, 1
Common Sorting Algorithms
Bubble Sort – Repeatedly compares and swaps adjacent elements until the list is sorted.
Selection Sort – Finds the smallest element and places it in the correct position.
Insertion Sort – Inserts each element into its proper place in the sorted portion of the list.
Merge Sort – Divides the list into halves, sorts them, and then merges them.
Quick Sort – Selects a pivot element and partitions the list around it, then recursively sorts the partitions.
Advantages of Sorting
Makes searching faster (e.g., Binary Search).
Organizes data efficiently.
Improves performance of many algorithms.
Simplifies data analysis and reporting.
Applications of Sorting
Arranging student records by roll number or marks.
Organizing names alphabetically.
Sorting products by price in online shopping.
Ranking players by score.
Managing databases efficiently.

Example:

Original List: 8, 3, 6, 1, 5
Ascending: 1, 3, 5, 6, 8
Descending: 8, 6, 5, 3, 1

Conclusion:
Sorting is a fundamental concept in computer science that arranges data in a meaningful order. It improves efficiency in searching, processing, and managing data, making it an essential technique in programming and database applications.
